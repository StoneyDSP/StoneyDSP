# How AI is used in StoneyDSP

StoneyDSP began as a personal software project long before the rise of
generative AI and large language models. Its repository history provides
direct evidence of that provenance.

I am a professional software developer with many years of experience before
AI-assisted development became commonplace. I do not require AI to design,
implement, understand, maintain, or release this codebase. StoneyDSP's
architecture, DSP work, coding style, platform ambitions, and product
direction are the result of my own technical decisions and experience.

AI is an optional engineering aid that I sometimes use to increase throughput
or reduce mechanical effort. It is not the source of the project's identity,
ownership, architecture, or technical authority.

## How AI is useful here

The most valuable uses are:

- optimised typing and implementation of work I have already specified;
- research assistance and comparison against documentation or source code;
- inspection of large logs, compiler output, and repository state;
- mechanical refactors and repetitive edits;
- cross-checking assumptions against the actual current code;
- verification and validation assistance, including running tests and builds;
- drafting documentation, work notes, commit messages, and review material.

These uses support my work; they do not replace the knowledge, judgement, or
responsibility required to do it.

## What AI does not decide

AI does not decide the architecture of StoneyDSP, its C or C++ APIs, its DSP
behaviour, supported platforms, package strategy, licensing, products,
commercial direction, or release criteria.

It does not determine whether an emulation is faithful, whether a DSP model
sounds correct, whether a plugin feels right in a host, or whether a result is
ready to ship. Those judgements remain human responsibilities.

## Verification and authority

The working relationship is:

1. I define the problem, intent, constraints, and acceptance criteria.
2. AI may research, propose, type, or check work within that boundary.
3. I inspect and evaluate the result against the actual code and requirements.
4. Builds, tests, runtime checks, listening tests, and target validation are
   performed as appropriate.
5. I decide whether anything is committed, pushed, merged, or released.

AI output is provisional until reviewed. AI may be wrong, incomplete, or
overconfident, and its conclusions are corrected when they conflict with the
codebase or my technical judgement.

AI is a tool in the development process, not a co-owner, architect, developer
of record, or substitute for professional software engineering.

## Why I use AI

I am a professional software engineer who already spends much of each working
day writing, reviewing, and maintaining software. Outside that work, I retain a
strong personal interest in audio DSP and a desire to contribute to the wider
community and help raise the standard of quality in the fields I care about.

Those interests exist alongside ordinary responsibilities: family, household
tasks, rest, and the need to maintain a sustainable life outside a screen.
Time is therefore a real constraint.

AI can reduce my development cycle time substantially, especially when the work
involves repetitive implementation, routine repository maintenance, or other
mechanical tasks. It helps me spend more of my limited personal time on the
parts of the work that require direct experience and judgement.

That distinction matters particularly for DSP. When researching, validating,
refining, or evaluating DSP behaviour, I remain deeply and directly involved.
I examine the assumptions, mathematics, implementation, measurements, audio
results, and trade-offs myself. AI may assist with research or verification,
but it does not replace the careful technical scrutiny required to produce
work I am willing to stand behind.

The purpose is not to avoid doing the work. It is to make enough time for the
work possible.
